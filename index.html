01 / WHO WE ARE

A COMMUNITY.
A COLLECTIVE VOICE.

The Assam Tea Tribes Students Association (ATTSA) stands as the historic intellectual shield and democratic platform of over seven million plantation laborers and their descendants. We exist to systematically bridge educational disparities, protect land and civic rights, and document the profound cultural heritage rooted within the Brahmaputra and Barak river valleys.

From the pioneering generation that organized the earliest garden study circles to modern university halls across the subcontinent, our mission unites community defense with visionary academic upliftment.

ERA 1947 – 1959

The Genesis & Early Awakening

Documentary Record Series I

Following India's independence, progressive students from diverse tea garden lines recognized that geographic isolation and exploitative indentured plantation frameworks could only be challenged through unified student organization. Initial community committees were formed across Dibrugarh, Jorhat, and Golaghat.

history_edu Archival Note: The earliest handwritten charter focused explicitly on evening vernacular schools and adult literacy circles.
ERA 1968 – 1985

Democratic Consolidation

Statewide Institutionalization

Formally adopting a unified constitution, ATTSA consolidated regional student guilds into a cohesive statewide body. The association initiated systematic dialogues with state authorities demanding the implementation of the Plantations Labour Act (PLA) 1951, dignified minimum wages, and statutory school construction in every tea estate.

gavel Milestone: State legislative delegations officially accepted ATTSA as the primary interlocutor for tea community welfare.
ERA 1990 – 2012

Expanding Horizons

The Hostel & Scholarship Movement

A decade marked by large-scale educational infrastructure mobilization. ATTSA spearheaded statewide campaigns establishing dedicated Tea Tribes student hostels across Guwahati, Dibrugarh, Tezpur, and Silchar, while securing post-matric scholarship allocations and university reservation frameworks.

CONSTITUTIONAL JURISPRUDENCE

Scheduled Tribe (ST) Recognition Charter

The Core Constitutional Disparity

The communities constituting Assam’s Tea Tribes (Santhal, Munda, Oraon, Kharia, Bhumij, etc.) are recognized as Scheduled Tribes (ST) in their states of origin (Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh). Due to colonial relocation to Assam, they were categorized under Other Backward Classes (OBC), denying them statutory safeguards under Article 342.

Key Demands of the Resolution:

  • Parliamentary Amendment: Enactment of presidential notification under Article 342 granting Scheduled Tribe (ST) status without dilution of existing tribal rights.
  • Permanent Land Pattas: Granting statutory, inheritable land titles (Myadi Patta) to workers for the homesteads they have inhabited for generations inside garden lines.
  • Fair Minimum Wage Parity: Implementation of the statutory living wage pegged to inflation and industrial parity under the Minimum Wages Act.
Document Ref: ATTSA/CONST/ART-342
COMMUNITY WELFARE OVERSIGHT

Tea Garden Healthcare Standards Charter

Under the Plantations Labour Act (PLA) 1951, garden managements are statutorily mandated to provide free, round-the-clock medical care, qualified doctors, and clean drinking water to laborers and families.

1. Maternal Health & Anemia Control

Mandatory nutritional supplements and paid maternity rest to combat maternal and infant mortality.

2. 24/7 Dispensaries with MBBS Doctors

Elimination of unqualified medical practice in garden dispensaries and dedicated emergency ambulance connectivity to district civil hospitals.

3. Potable Tap Water & Sanitation

Surveillance of water filtration plants to prevent waterborne epidemics and gastroenteritis outbreaks.

OFFICIAL RESOLUTION • CEC/RES/2024-88

12-Point Educational Resolution Report

Date of Ratification: October 24, 2024 Venue: District Auditorium, Dibrugarh Delegates in Attendance: 1,248

The Central Executive Committee (CEC) of ATTSA convened an extraordinary statewide educational convention to review the critical shortages in secondary education infrastructure across Assam tea estates.

Summary of Core Enacted Resolutions:

1. Mandatory Secondary Upgradation: Urgent conversion of 100 lower primary schools situated within interior garden lines into full-fledged secondary schools before the upcoming academic session.
2. Science & Math Faculty Appointments: Filling of 450 long-vacant permanent positions for science and mathematics teachers in tea garden high schools.
3. Universal Free Textbooks & Mid-Day Meal Quality: Strict auditing of mid-day meal nutritional standards and prompt distribution of bilingual study modules.
4. University Reservation Quota Expansion: Guaranteeing 10% reserved seats in higher education and technical institutes for tea tribe students.
